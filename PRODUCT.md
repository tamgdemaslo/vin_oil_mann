# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Eco-platform is used during the working day by an auto service owner, administrators, and mechanics. Owner users monitor money, profit, shifts, payroll, suppliers, inventory, CRM, and daily operations. Administrators work with appointments, client cases, shipments, cash, inventory operations, and messages. Mechanics start shifts, create and edit shipments, select oil and filters by VIN, and complete diagnostics.

The public booking surface is used by car owners, most often from a phone and often while multitasking. They need to understand the minimum required information, see genuinely available times, and complete a booking without calling or creating an account.

## Product Purpose

The platform brings daily auto service operations into one internal web interface: appointments, CRM, shipments, cash, inventory, finance, diagnostics, payroll, and customer communication. Its public surfaces turn that operational data into a clear client journey. Success means an employee quickly understands what needs action and a client can confidently book a real service slot in a few minutes.

## Brand Personality

Operational, clear, disciplined, and trustworthy. The interface should feel like a professional workbench for a busy service desk: dense enough for real work, calm enough to scan quickly, and direct about urgent items.

Public client journeys should feel confident, fast, and technologically clear while preserving the same disciplined voice.

## Anti-references

The product should not feel like a decorative SaaS dashboard, a generic CRM form, or a menu of every possible module. Avoid oversized hero blocks, equal-weight card grids, repeated alerts, duplicated KPI noise, low-contrast form copy, unnecessary steps, and secondary widgets that hide the task.

## Design Principles

1. Lead with today's work: appointments, client cases, shipments, and profit are the default home-page hierarchy.
2. Show action, not inventory: warnings and secondary systems stay compact unless they require immediate work.
3. Make status legible at a glance: arrival, on-site, in-work, left, unpaid, overdue, and missing shipment states must be visible without digging.
4. Preserve operational density: tables, timelines, and short rows are preferred over spacious marketing cards.
5. Keep role and shift constraints explicit: locked or unavailable work should explain what is blocked and where to start.
6. Make public journeys mobile-first: ask only for information needed at the current moment, keep the next action visible, and show progress in plain language.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast for operational text and controls. Keep motion minimal and state-based, respect reduced-motion preferences, preserve keyboard-visible focus states, and do not rely on color alone for warnings, payment status, or appointment status.
